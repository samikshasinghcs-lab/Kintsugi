# Kintsugi user-testing protocol

## Purpose
Evaluate whether people can identify a safe next step in the suspected-fraud and payment-hardship journeys. This is usability research, not financial advice or a test of real banking outcomes.

## Participant safety
- Use adults who freely consent; do not recruit anyone currently experiencing an active scam or acute financial distress.
- Use fictional scenarios only. Never ask participants to disclose real account, card, transaction, debt, health, or identity information.
- Tell participants the site is a demonstration and cannot contact banks, block cards, file claims, or arrange payment relief.
- Allow participants to stop at any time without explanation.
- Do not collect names, phone numbers, email addresses, recordings, or free-text personal stories.
- If a participant spontaneously discloses an active scam, stop the study and direct them to contact their bank using its official app or the number printed on their card.

## Suggested sample
Run a small formative round with 5–8 adults, then a second round after fixes. This is a usability sample, not statistically representative evidence.

## Tasks
1. Find what to do if a caller asks you to move money to a "safe account".
2. Find the next step after you transferred money because of deception.
3. Find what to do if your income stopped and a bill is due.
4. Find how to prepare to ask a provider for hardship support.
5. Find the human-support route and explain whether a handoff actually happened.

## Measures
Record only anonymous task-level measures:
- task completion (yes/no)
- time to identify the primary next step
- number of wrong turns
- whether participant understood the product cannot perform bank actions
- whether participant understood recovery/relief is not guaranteed
- confidence rating from 1–5
- observed accessibility or language barriers

## Success criteria (proposed, not measured)
- At least 4/5 participants identify official bank contact as the first action in active-fraud tasks.
- At least 4/5 understand the prototype does not block or reverse transactions.
- At least 4/5 understand hardship options are requests, not promises.
- No participant believes a case was submitted when only a summary was prepared.

## Reporting
Publish sample size, dates, task wording, aggregate results, observed limitations and changes made. Do not extrapolate a small convenience sample to all customers.
