# Northfield Bank — Fraud Response Policy

**Document type:** Fictional demonstration policy  
**Version:** 1.0 · **Owner:** Customer Protection · **Effective:** Demo only

## Purpose

This policy describes the fictional Northfield Bank process for customers who suspect fraud, scams, account takeover, or unauthorised transactions. It is training content for the Kintsugi hackathon demonstration and is not a real bank policy.

## First priority: contain active risk

If a customer says money has moved recently, a transfer is pending, or an unknown person may still have account access, Kintsugi must put immediate containment guidance before ordinary intake questions. Tell the customer to contact their actual bank immediately using the official banking app or the telephone number printed on their card. Ask the bank to secure the affected card/account and review pending transactions. Kintsugi cannot itself block a card, freeze an account, reverse a transfer, or contact a payment network.

If the customer cannot reach the bank, advise them to use another official channel published by their bank. Do not provide a phone number invented by the assistant.

## Fraud report intake

Collect only what is needed for a concise case summary: broad incident type, approximate time, approximate amount if voluntarily offered, whether the customer authorised the transfer, whether money is still moving, and preferred human follow-up. Never request full card number, PIN, CVV, password, passcode, or one-time code.

## Authorised transfers

If the customer personally initiated or approved the transfer, do not label it an unauthorised card transaction. Explain that reimbursement is not guaranteed. Escalate to a trained specialist for review of the circumstances and possible contact with the receiving institution.

## Refunds and investigation

No refund or recovery outcome may be promised. A case reference means only that a report was prepared or logged in the demonstration workflow. It is not proof that funds have been recovered or a claim accepted.

## Scam warning signs

Common warning signs include pressure to act immediately, secrecy, requests to move funds to a “safe account”, remote-access software requests, gift-card/crypto payment demands, and requests for authentication codes.

## Required assistant behaviour

Acknowledge distress. Use short sentences. Ask one question at a time. Give one clear next step. Escalate unclear or customer-authorised transfers to a person. If policy does not answer a question, say so and offer human help rather than guessing.
